## Session 2 — the cost of error
- Which cost-of-error line is a **legal or reputational** risk, not just money? **Legal & payroll-rule deadlines** (a wrong DSN/URSSAF or leave-rule deadline can make the customer file late and pay a penalty — and the error depends on jurisdiction/collective agreement), and **pay figures** (a wrong amount quoted to an HR manager can end in a labour-law dispute and a public loss of trust). That is why both are out of scope in the base case (`02_sizing.xlsx`, Include = 0); legal deadlines enter only with a jurisdiction check, in the optimistic scenario.
- Who in the company **owns** that risk? The **Head of Legal & Compliance** owns the legal-deadline content and its validation; the **Head of Support** owns the agent and the escalation rule; the DPO owns the data line.

### Trust level per step
| Step | Level | Why (cost of an error) |
|---|---|---|
| Read the ticket and classify | L4 above a confidence threshold, otherwise escalate | A wrong route is cheap if the policy check follows, expensive if a legal or pay ticket is treated as how-to (€300–500). |
| Retrieve passages | L3 | An irrelevant passage leads to a wrong answer, caught by citations and sampling. |
| Draft the answer from the passages | L3 on "how do I…" (5 % sampled), person only on everything else | A wrong how-to costs a reopened ticket (~€8). |
| Policy check (no amount, deadline or refund) | L4 — a rule, it can only block | A missed block costs €300–500 and a contract risk. |
| Send the answer | L3 on "how do I…", person only otherwise | Same. |
| Handle escalations | Person only | Money, law and emotion. |
## Session 4 — human-in-the-loop points and the override switch
- **Human checkpoints ⊘, and their kind:**
  - *abstention* — the router (below 0.70), the guard (guard words, human-only class, language, no passage) and the policy check hand the ticket over by themselves;
  - *approval* — in the prototype every draft is approved by the agent before sending (L2); every escalated ticket is handled by a person;
  - *sampling* — in production on "how do I…" (L3), 10 % of answered tickets are read by an agent each day.
- **Override switch ⏻:** the Head of Customer Support (or a delegate) can move the agent to suggest-only or off. In the prototype the switch is real (`prototype/switch.py`, scope: everyone); category and customer scopes are design only. **Tested on 16, 20 and 26 November 2026** (`prototype/test_switch.py`, log `data/switch_log.jsonl`), see Session 7.
- **Eval data:** `evals/` in this private repository; visible to the five team members and the teachers; synthetic, no personal data. Labels were hashed before the first run (`evals/LABELS_FROZEN.txt`).
- **What the first eval showed (governance relevance):** two human-only tickets received a draft (E27 no article, E50 how-to + minimum wage). The guard topics list is therefore a governance object: it needs an owner (payroll specialists / Legal) and a review cycle — see `spec.md` v2.

## Session 5 — counter-metrics and the segments we report
- **Counter-metrics:** the 7-day reopen rate of answered tickets and the CSAT of agent-answered against human-answered tickets, read in the Q1 pilot (`05_metrics.md` §3).
- **Segments reported:** language (English, French) · plan (Scale, Start) · category (answerable, human-only) · month-end against the rest of the month.
- **Fairness metric:** equal error rates across segments (wrong-answer rate and escalation rate per language and per plan); no segment above the 4 % wrong-answer threshold.
- **Harm threshold that stops the rollout, and its owner:** in any language × plan segment, one amount leak in the sample, or a 7-day reopen rate above the human baseline for two weeks in a row, or a wrong-answer rate above 4 %, sends the agent back to the previous adoption step; the **Head of Customer Support** decides on the switch ⏻.
## Session 6 — protected segments and the harm threshold that stops an experiment
- **Experiments on real users.** Any experiment on real customers is signed by the Head of Customer Support and read by the DPO before it starts; the decision rule and the harm threshold (Session 5 line above) are written before the first unit (`06_07_experiments_operations.md` §2). Segments pre-registered: language, plan, category, month-end against the rest of the month.

## Session 8 — who signs the launch, and what we ship
- **The launch decision.** The Head of Customer Support signs any move of the agent towards customers (pilot, adoption step), on the evidence of a new eval run; the CFO reads it against the hurdle (150 k€). On 26 November 2026 the answer is **no customer-facing launch** (`08_launch_memo.md`): 29 % sendable drafts against 45 %, and 4 drafts on person-only tickets against a floor of 0.
- **What ships in `v1.0`.** The prototype in **suggest-only**, never in front of a customer; the switch tested a third time on 26 November 2026 (passed). The floor stays a floor: no release, at any step, with one draft on a person-only ticket or one amount leak on the eval-set.
- **The grading rule is a governance object too.** Two-question tickets with a pay or legal part are person only as a whole (E95, `evals/README.md`): the rule that protects the floor wins over the rule that raises the score.
